Name

Why bluepuff

Bluepow joins a sky colour to the thing skiers chase. The same pattern for racers is blue plus the thing they chase on the water. Bluepuff was chosen over the options shown here and over bluesail, which is harder to trademark and says sailing rather than racing. It still needs a trademark and app-store search before launch.

CHOSEN · 9 NOV 2026
bluepuff

A puff is the gust racers hunt for upwind ("puff coming down the left"). Same shape as bluepow: blue, then one short p-word. Reads as an obvious sibling and is easy to say on the dock.

Risk: it sounds light-hearted. The serious tone has to come from the numbers and the copy, as it does for Bluepow.

blueshift

Wind shifts decide most dinghy races, and the word already means something. It sounds more technical and more grown-up.

Risk: a crowded name. Blueshift is already a marketing software company and a common product name, so the trademark and store search may block it.

bluepow sail

One brand with two sports. Uses Bluepow's existing name, store listing and social accounts, and one Plus subscription could cover both.

Risk: "pow" means powder. It reads oddly to sailors who don't ski, and it ties the sailing product to a winter brand.

The palette follows Bluepow's token for token, so the two apps can share code: one full-bleed blue, two darker blues for surfaces and night, three pale tints for text, and one yellow. The blue moves from Bluepow's violet-leaning cobalt toward the sea. Two sailing colours are added, and only for tack advice.

Rough share of a typical screen. If yellow covers more than a race-on chip and one number, something else has borrowed it.

bluepow Cobalt #0B2BB0 · winter, high-altitude sky
bluepuff Regatta #0A4DA8 · summer, open water
  • Port red and starboard green follow the navigation-light convention every sailor knows. They mark tack-specific advice ("port pays after 13:00") and nothing else: never errors, never success states.
  • Never rely on red and green alone, since about 1 in 12 men can't tell them apart. The word Port or Starboard is always printed on the chip.
  • Wind-strength charts use one blue ramp from Spray (light air) to Abyss (strong), with your fleet's range outlined in Sun. No rainbow wind maps.

  • Wind speed in knots by default. Offer m/s and Beaufort in settings, never mixed on one screen.
  • Direction as a compass point plus degrees ("SW 225°"): the point is quick to read, the degrees are what racers plot shifts with.
  • Times in the club's local 24-hour time, tied to the gun: "first gun 11:00", not "late morning".
Voice

A good club coach on the dock

Bluepow speaks like a friend who checked the models so you didn't have to. Bluepuff speaks like the coach on the dock before the start: short, specific, in the racers' own terms, and straight about how sure it is.

Principles

  • Odds, never promises. Every call carries a percentage. Bluepow's rule, unchanged.
  • Lead with the start. Racers care about the wind at the gun, then how it changes over the races.
  • Speak sailor, correctly. Puffs, lifts, headers, sea breeze, fleet, gun. A misused term costs more trust than a missed forecast.
  • Show the record. Publish every hit and miss, and say where a number comes from until the live record has enough races.
  • No hype. No "epic", no "send it", no "perfect conditions". Let 74% do the talking.

Words we use

Race on · the wind is in your fleet's chosen range at the first gun. The sail app's bluebird.
Puff · a gust, given as the peak speed in knots ("puffs to 17 kn"). Not "gust" in the app.
Shift · a change in wind direction of 10° or more. Say left or right, and which tack it lifts.
Sea breeze · thermal onshore wind, given as odds and a fill time ("61% by 13:00").
Fleet range · the lowest and highest wind a racer wants for their class, set once. ILCA, 420, RS200 and Optimist come with defaults.
First gun · the club's warning signal time. Every forecast is pinned to it.
